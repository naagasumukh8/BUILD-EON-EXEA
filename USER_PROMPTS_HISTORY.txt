Direct Alternate Route — bypass Hormuz via another maritime route, such as Cape of Good Hope or another feasible corridor.
Pipeline Bypass — move crude through an available pipeline and then re-ship from a suitable terminal.
Replacement Supply — buy equivalent product from another origin/market instead of moving the affected cargo.
Moving Vessel Opportunity — identify a vessel already travelling near a useful corridor and explore whether it can serve the requirement.
Cargo / Delivery Swap — exchange delivery obligations between two parties so neither side has to make an inefficient physical movement.
Local / Regional Exchange — use equivalent inventory already near the destination instead of transporting your own cargo.
Backhaul Opportunity — use a vessel's otherwise empty or underutilized return journey to move another cargo.
Triangulation — connect multiple cargoes/ports/voyages so one movement feeds another and reduces ballast or repositioning.
Transshipment — transfer cargo between vessels or modes at a suitable hub to avoid an inefficient direct movement.
Hybrid Strategy — combine multiple modes/opportunities, e.g. pipeline + vessel + replacement supply.
Wait / Timing Strategy — delay transit until a feasible/safe window rather than immediately committing to an expensive alternative.
Local Inventory / Stock Substitution — obtain product from existing regional inventory or another company's nearby stock.
Counterparty Exchange / Cargo Swap — a more explicit commercial version of #5 where two companies exchange physical positions or delivery obligations.
Diversified Split — deliberately divide the requirement across several independent sources/routes to reduce concentration and disruption risk.
Alternative Origin Strategy — change the loading origin entirely when the original origin depends on Hormuz.
Alternative Destination / Discharge Strategy — discharge somewhere feasible and move the product onward through another network.
STS / Lightering Strategy — ship-to-ship transfer where operationally, legally, and commercially feasible.
Multi-modal Strategy — combine maritime + pipeline + road/rail/terminal infrastructure where applicable.
Demand / Allocation Rebalancing — change which destination, refinery, or business unit receives which available cargo.
Emergency Replacement + Stranded Cargo Strategy — procure replacement supply now while treating the disrupted cargo as a separate future position.
